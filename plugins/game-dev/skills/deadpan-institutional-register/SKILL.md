# Deadpan Institutional Register

**Purpose:** Write the voice of an organisation that has filed the supernatural
under the wrong heading and moved on.

**Sources:** The tradition runs through *Catch-22* (Heller, 1961) for procedural
absurdity played straight, *The Twilight Zone*'s corporate episodes, Douglas
Adams's Vogons for bureaucracy as horror, Charles Stross's *Laundry Files* for
occult-as-civil-service, and *Welcome to Night Vale* for institutional calm about
the impossible. In games: *Papers, Please*, *Universal Paperclips*, *Control*,
*The Stanley Parable*. Slot rules below are derived from those and from a working
implementation; they are practice, not findings.

**This skill is tone only.** Structure — passage length, teaching copy, the
information-state ledger — is in **game-narrative-craft**. A perfectly deadpan
line that teaches the wrong thing at the wrong time is still a broken line.

---

## Slot 1 — The scale rule

**The threat is supernatural-large. The frame is administratively-small. The
frame never yields.**

This inverts cosmic horror, where the threat is cosmic and the payoff is a small
human warmth. Here the *institution* is the constant, and the joke is that it
absorbs the impossible without adjusting. Wolves on the site are logged as
weather. The moon is a known variable. Hazard pay remains under review.

The horror and the comedy are the same fact: **nothing the player encounters is
big enough to change the paperwork.** A player who dies has failed to meet a
quota.

**Test:** name the largest thing that happens in your game, then write the form
it would be filed under. If the form would be *revised* by the event, you have
written a different genre — one where the institution can be surprised, which
means it can be reasoned with, which removes the dread.

---

## Slot 2 — The signature move

**The category error, played absolutely straight.**

The institution processes the impossible using the wrong instrument and does not
notice. Not "the company is evil" — evil is engaged. **Indifference through
correct procedure** is the register.

| Move | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Misfiling** | The supernatural entered under a mundane heading | *"Wolves on the cut. Logged as weather."* |
| **The pending review** | A catastrophe awaiting an administrative decision | *"Hazard pay remains under review."* |
| **The unchanged rate** | Reality shifts; the terms do not | *"Blood moon. Rates unchanged."* |
| **Awareness without action** | Someone above knows and has done nothing | *"Corporate is aware of the moon."* |
| **The policy citation** | A cosmic constraint given a mundane authority | *"Cutting rights expire at dawn. Company policy."* |

**Rule: the institution is never the villain and never the fool.** A villain is
opposable. A fool is correctable. What makes this register work is that the
organisation is *functioning correctly* and the correctness is the problem.

**Test:** could a competent, well-meaning employee have written this line in good
faith? If it reads as authored by someone in on the joke, it has broken.

## Slot 3 — Sensory palette

**Leads with:** units, rates, quantities, deadlines, forms, job titles,
equipment nomenclature, the language of scheduling and entitlement. The texture
of work — the specific weight of a thing, the specific hour.

**Underuses:** gore, fear, the monster's interiority, weather as mood, anything
the institution would not have a field for. **The register cannot describe what
it cannot file**, and that constraint is the whole effect.

**Signature moves:**

- **The precise number in the wrong place.** *"120 wood, carried, reopens it."*
  Specificity where you expect a reaction.
- **Passive voice with an absent actor.** *"Logged as weather"* — by whom? The
  omission is the horror; this is the one register where passive voice is
  correct, and it is a deliberate exception to the rule in
  **game-narrative-craft**.
- **The reassurance that reassures nobody.** *"Rates unchanged."*
- **Equipment named by function, never by menace.** A torch is lighting. An axe
  is issued.

**Vocabulary that carries it:** allowance, requisition, tonnage, logged, rate,
standard, entitlement, review, policy, on file, per schedule, remains, pending,
issued, cleared, restocks.

## Slot 4 — What is never explained

**Never explain:**

- **Whether anyone above knows.** *"Corporate is aware of the moon"* is perfect
  because it confirms awareness and withholds everything else. Resolve this and
  the register collapses into either conspiracy or farce.
- **What the monsters are.** They are a condition of the site, like frost.
- **Why the player is still employed.** Economic necessity is understood and
  never stated. Stating it makes the game about that, and it isn't.
- **The joke.** Ever. This is the register's single hardest rule and the one that
  breaks most often under pressure to be funny.

**Do explain, exactly:** the rules, the rates, the quota, the schedule, what
counts and what does not. **The institution is scrupulously precise about
procedure and silent about meaning** — and that gap is where the player does the
work.

### The direction rule

**The farce points at the institution. Never at the monsters, and never at the
player.**

The wolves are not staff. They are not on a rota, they do not have opinions
about management, they are not secretly unionised. The moment a monster becomes
a participant in the joke, the horror leaves and does not return — you have
written a workplace comedy with a wolf in it.

**Test:** for each line, name its target. If the answer is the wolves, cut it. If
the answer is the player, cut it harder — this register never winks.

## Slot 5 — Register and period

**Default:** mid-century industrial memo. Clipped declaratives. Passive where a
memo would be passive. No contractions in official voice; contractions permitted
where a person is speaking rather than the company.

**Two voices, and they must be distinguishable at a glance:**

| Voice | Who | Style |
|---|---|---|
| **The company** | Notices, signage, terms, anything official | Imperative or declarative. Present tense. No hedging, no humour, no acknowledgement of the absurd. Often typographically distinct — small caps, all caps, a form-like layout. |
| **The wry aside** | Ambient scroll, incidental text | Sentence case. Slightly weary. Permitted to notice, never to complain. Still never explains. |

The gap between them carries most of the comedy. The company cannot see the joke;
the aside can see it and can do nothing.

**Calibration:**

- Sentences are short. A memo does not build to anything.
- Understatement always. The register never raises its voice — which is what
  makes the one line that does land.
- No modern idiom, no exclamation marks, no emoji-adjacent tone.
- **No jokes with a structure.** No setups, no punchlines, no rule-of-three. The
  humour is entirely situational and must survive being read flatly aloud by
  someone who does not find it funny.

**The inherited problem.** This register's raw material is real institutional
harm — dangerous work, absent employers, people with no option to leave. Played
carelessly it becomes a joke at the expense of the worker, which is both cruel
and unfunny. **Locate the absurdity in the institution's procedures, never in the
worker's predicament.** The player character is competent and stuck; that is
sympathetic. A player character who is stupid for staying is the register
failing.

## Slot 6 — Tuning forks

Read a page before drafting, for cadence:

- **Heller, *Catch-22*** — the founding text for procedure as horror. Read any
  Milo Minderbinder passage.
- **Real safety notices and terms of service.** Better than any fiction for the
  actual voice. A workplace hazard sign is doing this register sincerely.
- **Stross, *The Atrocity Archives*** — occult under civil-service management.
- **Night Vale** — institutional calm about the impossible, though it winks more
  than this register permits; read for the calm, not the jokes.

**Rule: read the sincere source, not the parody.** The parodies have already made
the choices you need to make yourself, and copying them yields a copy of a joke.

## Review checklist

Run after the **game-narrative-craft** checklist, not instead of it.

- [ ] Could a competent employee have written this in good faith?
- [ ] Is the target of the farce the institution — not the monsters, not the player?
- [ ] Is anything explained that should stay unexplained, especially the joke?
- [ ] Are the rules, rates and quantities stated with total precision?
- [ ] Company voice and aside voice distinguishable at a glance?
- [ ] Any line with a setup-and-punchline structure? Cut it.
- [ ] Does it survive being read aloud flatly by someone unamused?
- [ ] Is the absurdity in the procedure rather than in the worker's predicament?
- [ ] Any exclamation marks? Any winking?
